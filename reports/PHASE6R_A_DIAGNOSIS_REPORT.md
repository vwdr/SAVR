## Main diagnosis

The negative outcome was not explained by one isolated bad task or a technical
failure. The evidence points to a controller that declares reuse too easily
and then compounds its own closed-loop distribution shift.

### 1. Offline FR replay underpredicted online reuse

| Setting | Offline skip | Online skip | Online minus offline | Success |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `savr-s25-h2` | 24.98% | 34.69% | +9.71 pp | 52/100 |
| `savr-s25-h4` | 24.98% | 43.29% | +18.31 pp | 21/100 |
| `savr-s25-h8` | 24.98% | 47.28% | +22.30 pp | 23/100 |

All nine online settings skipped more than their FR-replay estimate. Once a
reused visual representation changes an action, the resulting trajectory and
future gate inputs no longer follow the FR trace used for calibration.
Therefore FR replay is useful for screening but is not a reliable online
refresh-budget or safety oracle.

### 2. Camera averaging concealed wrist-camera changes

For `savr-s25-h2`, 374 of 783 reuse queries (47.77%) had at least one camera
whose image-change score individually exceeded the shared image threshold even
though the two-camera mean remained below it. The exceedances were:

- wrist camera: 372;
- third-person camera: 2.

This is direct evidence that averaging the cameras can cancel a localized,
task-relevant change. It does not prove every concealed wrist change caused a
failure, but it invalidates the mean as a conservative safety gate.

### 3. Early reuse was associated with much lower success

For the best setting:

| First reuse | Episodes | Success |
|---|---:|---:|
| no reuse | 3 | 3/3 (100.0%) |
| query 2 or earlier | 23 | 7/23 (30.4%) |
| query 3-4 | 42 | 18/42 (42.9%) |
| query 5 or later | 32 | 24/32 (75.0%) |

This association is task-dependent and is not a causal estimate. It does show
that treating all query positions as equally safe is unsupported.

### 4. The first reuse usually changed the action immediately

In 87 `savr-s25-h2` episodes, the SAVR and FR action-hash prefix was comparable
through the first reuse. In all 87, the first action-hash mismatch occurred
exactly at that first reuse query.

Ten task-4 episodes already had a hash mismatch at query 0 and cannot support
this comparison; three episodes performed no reuse. Hash mismatch establishes
that the predicted action changed, not the magnitude or whether the change
alone caused failure.

### 5. Many reuse decisions had almost no threshold safety margin

Among failed `savr-s25-h2` episodes:

- 91.7% contained a reuse with image score at least 90% of its threshold;
- 52.1% contained a reuse with action score at least 90% of its threshold.

The corresponding successful-episode fractions were 67.3% and 34.7%.
Threshold-crossing without hysteresis treats a barely-below-threshold query as
equally safe as a stable query far below all thresholds.

### 6. A two-query horizon was still aggressive

Every failed `savr-s25-h2` episode reached two consecutive reuse queries.
Because each OpenVLA-OFT query predicts an eight-action chunk, this setting can
produce two consecutive chunks—up to 16 control actions—from a stale projected
visual representation before the horizon forces refresh.

The action signal is also retrospective: it compares the two preceding
predicted chunks. It cannot determine whether the action about to be generated
is the one that newly requires a fresh view.

### 7. Safety varied sharply with scene geometry

All LIBERO-Spatial tasks use the same pick-and-place goal but vary the bowl's
location. Under `savr-s25-h2`, task success ranged from:

- task 1, bowl next to the ramekin: 1/10;
- task 5, bowl on the ramekin: 2/10;
- task 6, bowl next to the cookie box: 9/10.

This heterogeneity supports location- and interaction-sensitive gating rather
than a single global stability test.

## Ranked findings

### Directly observed

1. FR-replay skip estimates did not transfer to closed-loop execution.
2. Two-camera averaging concealed frequent wrist-camera threshold exceedance.
3. Earlier reuse, longer streaks, and near-threshold reuse were associated
   with worse outcomes.
4. The first comparable reuse immediately changed the action hash.
5. Even horizon 2 permitted an unsafe amount of consecutive stale-vision use.

### Plausible but not yet proven

1. Failures concentrate around grasp/contact/release transitions.
2. Global 32-by-32 mean pixel change dilutes small task-relevant object motion.
3. RMS state/action aggregation dilutes critical dimensions such as gripper
   state or a directional reversal.
4. A short stability history and explicit safety vetoes can preserve success
   while retaining useful reuse.

Phase 6 did not save raw online SAVR observations/actions, phase labels, or
videos. Those missing data prevent a definitive contact-level causal
classification and must be added to bounded redesign diagnostics.

## Required properties of SAVR 2.0

The redesign protocol should require:

1. independent per-camera safety thresholds or a max-camera veto;
2. isolated reuse followed by mandatory refresh, at least initially;
3. an early-trajectory warm-up and cooldown after every reuse;
4. stronger margin/hysteresis rather than bare threshold crossing;
5. explicit gripper/action-transition and direction-change veto candidates;
6. stability across multiple freshly observed queries before reuse;
7. online staged calibration with low initial reuse targets;
8. an online budget governor that fails conservatively toward FR;
9. raw bounded diagnostic signals and selected rollout videos;
10. mechanical promotion/stop rules that preserve all negative outcomes.

## Phase boundary

not implement the redesign or launch a calibration run. Phase 6R-B requires
